How to invoice a deposit and the balance due

Take a deposit before you start, invoice it, record what the client pays, then send a final invoice that subtracts the deposit and shows what is still owed — with notes on the UK VAT tax point for deposits.

02Deposit and balance in four steps

This is common practice for freelancers and small service businesses, not a rule of any one country:

  1. Agree the deposit in the quote — a percentage or a fixed amount, and when it is due.
  2. Send a deposit invoice for that amount, with its own invoice number.
  3. Record the payment when it arrives, including part payments, so you can see what is still open.
  4. Send the final invoice when the work is done: the full total, less each deposit (with its invoice number and date), and the balance due.

Invoice lays out the final invoice like this: the tax on the whole job, then each deposit deducted together with the tax it carried, so the client does not pay that tax twice. How the tax on a deposit and on the balance has to be shown depends on your country; for the UK, see the note under the final invoice.

03What to put on a deposit invoice

In the UK, GOV.UK's general list of what an invoice must include is:

  1. A unique invoice number
  2. Your business name, address and contact information, and your customer's name and address
  3. A description of what you are charging for — for a deposit, for example “Deposit of 30% on Quote QUO-2026-000012”
  4. The date of supply and the date of the invoice
  5. The amount charged, VAT if it applies, and the total amount owed

Sole traders and limited companies have a few extra items (for example, a limited company's full registered name); see the GOV.UK page.

A VAT invoice also needs, among other things, your VAT registration number, the time of supply (tax point), the date of issue if it differs from the tax point, for each line the quantity, the unit price, the VAT rate and the amount before VAT, and the total VAT in sterling (VAT Notice 700, 16.3.1). Other countries have their own lists.

A deposit invoice is usually issued before the work is done. For UK VAT, the tax point of the deposit is the invoice date or the payment date, whichever comes first (see UK VAT), so in Invoice don't enter the future job date as the service date. If you issue the deposit invoice before you are paid, leave it on “Same as issue date”. If the payment came first, ask your accountant which date to show.

04The final invoice: total, less deposit, balance due

The final invoice repeats the whole job, then subtracts every deposit the client has paid. Example: a £3,000 job plus 20% VAT, with a 30% deposit paid up front.

incl. VAT 20%£180.00

UK VAT note: the deposit and the balance each have their own tax point. Only the VAT on the balance (£420 in this example) is accounted for when the final invoice is issued; the “incl. VAT” line shows the VAT already invoiced on the deposit (VATTOS3600). Ask your accountant how to present this on your invoices.

Once the client pays part of an invoice, the invoice can show what was paid and the balance due, so the client sees the same number you do.

05UK VAT: deposits and the tax point

If you are VAT-registered in the UK, a deposit usually brings forward when you account for VAT on that part of the job. From VAT Notice 700, section 14:

DepositReceiving a deposit or issuing a VAT invoice for it, whichever comes first, creates a tax point for that amount.
14 daysA VAT invoice issued up to 14 days after the basic tax point usually becomes the tax point for the rest.
  • Basic tax point for services: the date the service is performed, normally when all the work except invoicing is done (14.2.1).
  • Advance payments: the deposit rule above applies to any payment received or VAT invoice issued before the basic tax point (14.2.2(a)).
  • Deposit invoice before payment: if you issue a VAT deposit invoice before you are paid, the tax point is the invoice date, and the VAT goes in the return for that period even if the client has not paid yet; you cannot wait for the payment (14.2.2). Issuing a pro forma, which is not a VAT invoice, does not create a tax point by itself; receiving the payment does.
  • Security deposits are different: a refundable deposit held, for example, against the safe return of hired goods is not payment for a supply and does not create a tax point (14.2.3).
  • The balance: the 14-day rule above applies to the balance; a deposit or part-payment invoice you issued earlier has already created its own tax point for its amount (14.2.2(b)). HMRC's worked example in VATTOS3600 shows two tax points on one job.
  • Kept or refunded deposits: VAT is due on deposits that are full or part payments, and still due if you keep the deposit because the customer does not go ahead. If you refund it, you can reclaim the VAT you accounted for in your next return (8.13.1).
  • Pro forma invoices: a pro forma used to ask for payment before the supply cannot be used to reclaim VAT and should be marked “This is not a VAT invoice”; once you supply or receive payment, issue a VAT invoice (Notice 700, 17.3).
  • Cash Accounting Scheme: HMRC lists “where a VAT invoice is raised in advance” among the cases the scheme cannot be used for. If you use the scheme and invoice deposits in advance, ask an accountant how this applies to you.

06US sales tax and other countries

In the US, sales tax is set state by state, and this guide does not cover how any state treats customer deposits. Check with your state's department of revenue or an accountant. The same goes for GST, HST and VAT outside the UK: the four steps above are common practice, but when tax is due on a deposit depends on where you are.

07Deposits in Invoice: good to know

  • You can request a deposit only on a quote marked as accepted, and only before an invoice exists for that quote. Several deposits are allowed; together they cannot exceed the quote total.
  • The final invoice deducts only deposit invoices that are issued and paid in full, each one once. Until at least one deposit is fully paid, the app does not create the final invoice.
  • On the free plan, every deposit invoice and the final invoice each count as one invoice toward the 3 free invoices: one deposit plus the final invoice uses 2 of them.
  • A payment cannot be more than the balance due, and its date cannot be in the future.
  • Deposit invoices made from a quote are invoices with their own invoice number, not pro formas. Invoice also has pro formas (Documents › Pro formas); a pro forma made in Invoice prints “This document is not an invoice.” and does not count toward the free invoices.
  • Invoice does not work out your tax point. You enter the service date where it applies.
  • Invoice cannot issue invoices for sellers in some countries (for example Spain and Italy), or business-to-business invoices in a few others (such as Poland, Croatia and Greece), so deposits and balances are not available in those cases.

Deposits and balances with Invoice

  1. Mark the quote as accepted and tap Deposit: choose a percentage (pre-filled with 30%) or a fixed amount. The app creates a draft deposit invoice with the quote's tax rates.
